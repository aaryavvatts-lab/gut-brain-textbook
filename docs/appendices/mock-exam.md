This mock exam has **50 multiple-choice questions** that cover all 20 chapters of *Gut Check*. Plan on about **60 minutes**. It works for self-study before a test, for review after you finish the book, or as a class exam that teachers can assign in full or by part.

**How to use it:**

- The questions are grouped into five parts that follow the order of the book. Each part covers four chapters.
- Choose the best answer for each question before you click **Show Answer**. Many questions ask you to read data, grade a claim with the Hype Meter, or predict what happens next in a pathway, so take your time.
- Each answer names the chapter it comes from, so you know where to review.
- The [Answer Key](#answer-key) at the end lists all 50 answers in one table for quick scoring.

This exam is for learning. It is not medical advice.

## Part I: Foundations of the Gut-Brain Axis (Chapters 1 to 4)

#### Question 1

Which nerve network inside the gut wall mainly controls the lining (secretion and local blood flow) rather than gut movement?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The myenteric plexus
2. The submucosal plexus
3. The nodose ganglion
4. The dorsal motor nucleus of the vagus
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. The submucosal plexus sits just under the lining and mainly works on the mucosa. The myenteric plexus, between the two muscle layers, mainly controls movement. The nodose ganglion and dorsal motor nucleus belong to the vagus pathway, not to the enteric nervous system.

#### Question 2

A researcher places a short piece of animal intestine, with no connection to the brain or spinal cord, in a warm nutrient bath and slips a small soft ball inside. What should she predict?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The wall squeezes behind the ball and relaxes ahead of it, pushing the ball along.
2. Nothing happens, because every gut movement needs a command from the brain.
3. The wall twitches at random but cannot move the ball in one direction.
4. The ball moves only if the vagus nerve is left attached to the piece.
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. The peristaltic reflex is a local enteric circuit: stretch triggers contraction behind the food and relaxation ahead of it. Scientists have watched isolated intestine do this since 1755. The brain and vagus can adjust gut movement, but they are not required for this reflex.

    **Chapter:** [Chapter 1: The Brain in Your Belly (and Why That Phrase Is Misleading)](../chapters/01-brain-in-your-belly/index.md)

#### Question 3

#### Question 5

An influencer measures her heart rate variability (HRV) before and after five minutes of humming. Her HRV goes up, and she posts: "Humming reset my vagus nerve and cured my anxiety!" Which critique is strongest?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
