# References: Celiac Disease and Food Reactions

1. [Definition & Facts for Celiac Disease](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/celiac-disease/definition-facts) - National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK) - The US government's plain-language overview of celiac disease, with linked pages on symptoms, causes (including the DQ2 and DQ8 gene figures), treatment, and eating. Source for the "about 2 million Americans" estimate and the difference between celiac disease, wheat allergy, and gluten sensitivity.

2. [Diagnosis of Celiac Disease](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/celiac-disease/diagnosis) - NIDDK - Explains the blood tests, small bowel biopsy, and genetic testing, and states that doctors don't recommend starting a gluten-free diet before testing. The best single page for Section 12.5.

3. [American College of Gastroenterology Guidelines Update: Diagnosis and Management of Celiac Disease](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36602836/) - Rubio-Tapia A, et al., *American Journal of Gastroenterology*, 2023 - The current US clinical guideline for doctors. Its summary describes celiac disease as a permanent immune response to gluten, tTG antibodies as the first screening test, biopsy for most patients, and a strict gluten-free diet with lifelong follow-up as treatment.

4. [Global Prevalence of Celiac Disease: Systematic Review and Meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29551598/) - Singh P, et al., *Clinical Gastroenterology and Hepatology*, 2018 - Pooled 96 studies to estimate that 1.4% of people worldwide test positive on blood tests and 0.7% have biopsy-confirmed celiac disease. A good example of how "about 1 in 100" is calculated.

5. [Celiac Disease](https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/condition/celiac-disease/) - MedlinePlus Genetics, US National Library of Medicine - Clear explanation of how celiac disease flattens the villi and how HLA-DQ gene variants raise the chance of an immune reaction to gluten, plus family risk figures.

6. [23.5 The Small and Large Intestines](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/23-5-the-small-and-large-intestines) - OpenStax, *Anatomy and Physiology 2e* (CC BY 4.0) - Free open textbook section on circular folds, villi, microvilli, and the brush border enzymes. Source for the villus sizes and the "more than 600-fold" surface increase in Section 12.2.

7. [Fructan, Rather Than Gluten, Induces Symptoms in Patients With Self-Reported Non-Celiac Gluten Sensitivity](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29102613/) - Skodje GI, et al., *Gastroenterology*, 2018 - A double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial of 59 adults that tested gluten and fructan separately. The data in Your Gut Check question 5 come from this study.

8. [Long-term Intake of Gluten and Cognitive Function Among US Women](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8140370/) - Wang Y, et al., *JAMA Network Open*, 2021 (free full text) - A cohort study of 13,494 women without celiac disease that found no link between gluten intake and thinking scores. The study analyzed in the chapter's claim box.

9. [Neurological Manifestations of Neuropathy and Ataxia in Celiac Disease: A Systematic Review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30759885/) - Mearns ES, et al., *Nutrients*, 2019 - Reviews 16 studies on peripheral neuropathy (reported in up to 39% of patients) and gluten ataxia (0% to 6%) in celiac disease. Useful for seeing how wide the range of estimates can be.

10. [Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Management of Food Allergy in the United States](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4241964/) - NIAID-Sponsored Expert Panel (Boyce JA, et al.), *Journal of Allergy and Clinical Immunology*, 2010 (free full text) - The expert definitions of food allergy, IgE-mediated and non-IgE reactions, and food intolerance used in Section 12.9. Long and technical; the definitions are in Section 2.1.

11. [Definition & Facts for Lactose Intolerance](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/lactose-intolerance/definition-facts) - NIDDK - Explains lactose intolerance and lactose malabsorption, how common they are worldwide and in the US, and why lactose intolerance is different from a milk allergy.

12. [Re-challenge Studies in Non-celiac Gluten Sensitivity: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28928668/) - Lionetti E, et al. *Frontiers in Physiology*, 2017 (free full text) - Pools 11 gluten rechallenge studies: about 30% of people with suspected NCGS relapsed on gluten, overall no different from placebo, but under the Salerno method more relapsed on gluten than on placebo (40% vs 24%). Source for Section 12.8.
