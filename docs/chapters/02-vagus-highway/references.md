# References: The Vagus Highway

1. [13.4 The Peripheral Nervous System](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/13-4-the-peripheral-nervous-system) - OpenStax, *Anatomy and Physiology 2e* (CC BY 4.0) - Free open textbook section on the twelve cranial nerves, with a table showing which are sensory, motor, or mixed. A clear review of where the vagus nerve (CN X) fits in Section 2.3.

2. [15.1 Divisions of the Autonomic Nervous System](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/15-1-divisions-of-the-autonomic-nervous-system) - OpenStax, *Anatomy and Physiology 2e* (CC BY 4.0) - Explains the sympathetic and parasympathetic branches, where their nerves leave the brainstem and spinal cord, and why "fight or flight" is an incomplete nickname.

3. [Internal senses of the vagus nerve](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35051375/) - Prescott SL, Liberles SD. *Neuron*, 2022 - Research review on how vagal sensory neurons detect stretch, nutrients, and other signals from the organs. Source for the "about 80% sensory" figure (traced to classic animal fiber counts, a 1937 cat study by Foley and DuBois), the nodose ganglion to NTS pathway, and the 1% share of enteroendocrine cells.

4. [The Vagus Nerve at the Interface of the Microbiota-Gut-Brain Axis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5808284/) - Bonaz B, Bazin T, Pellissier S. *Frontiers in Neuroscience*, 2018 - Open-access review describing the vagus as the main parasympathetic nerve, its 80/20 fiber split, how gut hormones signal through it, and the link between low vagal tone and IBD and IBS.

5. [A gut-brain neural circuit for nutrient sensory transduction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6417812/) - Kaelberer MM, et al. *Science*, 2018 - The mouse study that named neuropod cells, analyzed in this chapter's "How to read a gut-brain claim" box. The full text lists its experiments, funding, and patent disclosure.

6. [Extrinsic primary afferent signalling in the gut](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23438947/) - Brookes SJ, Spencer NJ, Costa M, Zagorodnyuk VP. *Nature Reviews Gastroenterology & Hepatology*, 2013 - Review describing the three sensory pathways from the gut (vagal and two spinal) and the sensations they produce, such as fullness, nausea, urgency, and pain. The abstract is a good summary.

7. [Spinal Afferent Innervation of the Colon and Rectum](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6288476/) - Brierley SM, Hibberd TJ, Spencer NJ. *Frontiers in Cellular Neuroscience*, 2018 - Open-access review of the spinal sensory nerves that reach the colon and rectum and how they give rise to discomfort, bloating, urgency, and pain. Background for Section 2.8.

8. [Vagal neurocircuitry and its influence on gastric motility](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5605144/) - Travagli RA, Anselmi L. *Nature Reviews Gastroenterology & Hepatology*, 2016 - Explains the brainstem "vago-vagal" loop between the NTS and the dorsal motor nucleus and how it controls the stomach, including changes after stress. Advanced, but the opening sections are readable.

9. [Heart Rate Variability and Cardiac Vagal Tone in Psychophysiological Research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5316555/) - Laborde S, Mosley E, Thayer JF. *Frontiers in Psychology*, 2017 - Explains what HRV can and can't tell us about vagal tone and why HRV results are easy to misread. Useful for judging claims made by wearables and wellness apps.

10. [Function and mechanisms of enteroendocrine cells and gut hormones in metabolism](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30760847/) - Gribble FM, Reimann F. *Nature Reviews Endocrinology*, 2019 - Review of how gut sensor cells release hormones such as GLP-1 in response to food, and how GLP-1-based medicines grew out of this research.

11. [A relic or still relevant: the narrowing role for vagotomy in the treatment of peptic ulcer disease](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24139666/) - Lagoo J, Pappas TN, Perez A. *American Journal of Surgery*, 2014 - Surgical review explaining why vagotomy was once common for ulcers and why acid-reducing medicines and the discovery of *H. pylori* made it rare.

12. [Transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation attenuates inflammatory bowel disease in children: a proof-of-concept clinical trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10583463/) - Sahn B, et al. *Bioelectronic Medicine*, 2023 - Small 16-week study of an ear stimulator in 22 young people aged 10 to 21 with IBD, cited in the At the Bedside case. Only the first 4 weeks had a sham comparison; the authors list their limits clearly.
