# References: Chemical Messengers and the Serotonin Myth

1. [Chapter 3: Synapses and Neurotransmitters](https://aaryavvatts-lab.github.io/neuroscience-textbook/chapters/03-synapses-and-neurotransmitters/): *From Neuron to Bedside* (book #1 in this series). A free refresher on how synapses work and on neurotransmitters such as serotonin, dopamine, GABA, and acetylcholine. Read it if Section 5.3 moved too fast.

2. [Does serotonin in the intestines make you happy?](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7659911/): Bektaş A, et al. *Turkish Journal of Gastroenterology*, 2020. A short, readable editorial explaining why gut serotonin can't reach the brain and why the "banana makes you happy" idea fails. The single best source for this chapter's main point.

3. [Serotonin signalling in the gut: functions, dysfunctions and therapeutic targets](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4048923/): Mawe GM, Hoffman JM. *Nature Reviews Gastroenterology & Hepatology*, 2013. Free full-text review of what gut serotonin does, how SERT clears it, how platelets store it, how SSRIs affect the gut, and how drugs targeting gut serotonin are being developed.

4. [Indigenous bacteria from the gut microbiota regulate host serotonin biosynthesis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4393509/): Yano JM, et al. *Cell*, 2015. The mouse study analyzed in this chapter's "How to read a gut-brain claim" box, showing that spore-forming bacteria raise the gut's own serotonin production. Free full text with funding details.

5. [Enterochromaffin cells are gut chemosensors that couple to sensory neural pathways](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28648659/): Bellono NW, et al. *Cell*, 2017. Shows that EC cells, less than 1% of gut lining cells, sense chemicals, are electrically excitable, and connect to sensory nerves. The abstract is a good summary of the "gut as sensor" idea.

6. [Influence of tryptophan and serotonin on mood and cognition with a possible role of the gut-brain axis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4728667/): Jenkins TA, et al. *Nutrients*, 2016. Open-access review explaining tryptophan as an essential amino acid and how it competes with other amino acids to cross the blood-brain barrier.

7. [Fluoxetine](https://medlineplus.gov/druginfo/meds/a689006.html): MedlinePlus, US National Library of Medicine. Plain-language drug information for a common SSRI, including how it works, its important warning about suicidal thoughts in young people, common side effects such as nausea and diarrhea, and why it shouldn't be stopped without a doctor.

8. [The serotonin theory of depression: a systematic umbrella review of the evidence](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35854107/): Moncrieff J, et al. *Molecular Psychiatry*, 2023 (online 2022). The much-debated review behind Section 5.10. The PubMed page also lists the published comments and critiques, a good way to see a scientific debate in action.

9. [GABA-modulating bacteria of the human gut microbiota](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30531975/): Strandwitz P, et al. *Nature Microbiology*, 2019. Lab and stool-sample evidence that common gut bacteria such as *Bacteroides* make GABA. Useful for separating "bacteria make it" from "it reaches your brain."

10. [The neuroactive potential of the human gut microbiota in quality of life and depression](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30718848/): Valles-Colomer M, et al. *Nature Microbiology*, 2019. A large human study (1,054 people plus validation data sets) linking certain gut bacteria to quality of life and depression, with a clear warning about confounding factors.

11. [The blood-brain barrier](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25561720/): Daneman R, Prat A. *Cold Spring Harbor Perspectives in Biology*, 2015. Review describing how the brain's blood vessel cells control what passes from blood into the brain. Background for Section 5.7 and Chapter 6.

12. [A leaky umbrella has little value: evidence clearly indicates the serotonin system is implicated in depression](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10618084/): Jauhar S, et al. *Molecular Psychiatry*, 2023. Free full-text reply to the 2022 umbrella review, written by 35 psychiatrists and brain scientists. Read it next to reference 8 to see both sides of the debate in Section 5.10.
