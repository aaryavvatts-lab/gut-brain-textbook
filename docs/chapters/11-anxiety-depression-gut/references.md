# References: Anxiety, Depression, and the Gut

1. [Prevalence of symptoms of anxiety and depression in patients with inflammatory bowel disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33721557/) - Barberio B, et al. *Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology*, 2021 - The 77-study, 30,118-adult meta-analysis behind this chapter's main numbers and its "How to read a gut-brain claim" box. The abstract gives the overall rates and the active-versus-inactive comparison.

2. [Systematic review with meta-analysis: the prevalence of anxiety and depression in patients with irritable bowel syndrome](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31157418/) - Zamani M, Alizadeh-Tabari S, Zamani V. *Alimentary Pharmacology & Therapeutics*, 2019 - Pools studies of people with IBS and finds anxiety and depression symptoms at about three times the odds seen in healthy people.

3. [Systematic review with meta-analysis: anxiety and depression in children and adolescents with inflammatory bowel disease](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29984495/) - Stapersma L, et al. *Alimentary Pharmacology & Therapeutics*, 2018 - The teen-focused meta-analysis (28 studies, average age 14.3) discussed in Section 11.4, with its cautions about different questionnaires and cutoffs.

4. [The brain-gut pathway in functional gastrointestinal disorders is bidirectional: a 12-year prospective population-based study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22234979/) - Koloski NA, et al. *Gut*, 2012 - A 12-year Australian cohort study showing that anxiety predicts new gut disorders and gut disorders predict later anxiety and depression. A classic example of a cohort study testing direction.

5. [Bi-directionality of brain-gut interactions in patients with inflammatory bowel disease](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29366841/) - Gracie DJ, Guthrie EA, Hamlin PJ, Ford AC. *Gastroenterology*, 2018 - A 2-year cohort of 405 adults with IBD showing that active disease predicts later anxiety and anxiety predicts later flares. Source for Section 11.6.

6. [Inflammatory markers in depression: a meta-analysis of mean differences and variability in 5,166 patients and 5,083 controls](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7327519/) - Osimo EF, et al. *Brain, Behavior, and Immunity*, 2020 - Free full text of the meta-analysis showing that, on average, people with depression have higher inflammation markers. Useful for weighing the inflammatory depression theory.

7. [Transferring the blues: depression-associated gut microbiota induces neurobehavioural changes in the rat](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27491067/) - Kelly JR, et al. *Journal of Psychiatric Research*, 2016 - One of the two microbiota transfer studies in Section 11.8. A good example of an animal experiment that tests cause but can't prove what happens in people.

8. [The neuroactive potential of the human gut microbiota in quality of life and depression](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30718848/) - Valles-Colomer M, et al. *Nature Microbiology*, 2019 - A large human study linking certain gut bacteria to depression and quality of life, while stressing the importance of confounders. Observational, so it can't show direction.

9. [Efficacy of psychological therapies in people with inflammatory bowel disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37543040/) - Riggott C, Mikocka-Walus A, Gracie DJ, Ford AC. *Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology*, 2023 (open access) - Updates the group's 2017 analysis (Gracie et al.) to 25 randomized trials: small short-term benefits for anxiety, depression, stress, and quality of life in quiet IBD, a small lasting benefit for depression scores, and no effect on disease activity. Source for the integrated care evidence in Section 11.11.

10. [Unmet Need for Mental Health Services Among High School Students Experiencing Persistent Feelings of Sadness or Hopelessness: Youth Risk Behavior Survey, United States, 2025](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/75/su/su7501a4.htm) - Kiplagat SJ, et al. (CDC), *MMWR Supplements* 75(1):35-43, October 15, 2026 - The newest national survey: 33.3% of US high school students reported persistent sadness or hopelessness in 2025, and about 3 in 10 of those students (28.8%) reported an unmet need for counseling or therapy.

11. [Teen Depression: More Than Just Moodiness](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/teen-depression) - National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH) - A short, free guide for teens on the signs of depression, how it is treated, and how to get help from a trusted adult, a doctor, or the 988 Lifeline.

12. [Get Help](https://988lifeline.org/get-help/) - 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline - How to reach the free, confidential, 24/7 Lifeline by call, text, or chat, including options in Spanish. Bookmark it, for yourself or for a friend.
