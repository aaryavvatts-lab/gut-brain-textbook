# References: Stress and the Gut

1. [17.6 The Adrenal Glands](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/17-6-the-adrenal-glands) - OpenStax, *Anatomy and Physiology 2e* (CC BY 4.0) - Free open textbook section on the adrenal cortex and medulla, the HPA axis, fight-or-flight, the general adaptation syndrome, and cortisol's effects on fuel and inflammation. The best starting point for Sections 7.3 to 7.7.

2. [Cortisol Test](https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/cortisol-test/) - MedlinePlus, US National Library of Medicine - A plain-language page on what cortisol does, how ACTH controls it, its daily rhythm (highest in the morning), and why both high and low cortisol can be serious.

3. [The role of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis in neuroendocrine responses to stress](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17290797/) - Smith SM, Vale WW. *Dialogues in Clinical Neuroscience*, 2006 - An open-access review of the HPA axis, CRH (CRF), and glucocorticoid negative feedback. Source for the cascade and feedback in Sections 7.4 and 7.6.

4. [Role of Corticotropin-releasing Factor Signaling in Stress-related Alterations of Colonic Motility and Hyperalgesia](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25611064/) - Taché Y, Million M. *Journal of Neurogastroenterology and Motility*, 2015 - Review of how CRF and its receptors in the brain and colon speed up colon movement and raise pain sensitivity in animals, and why CRF1-blocking drugs haven't yet become treatments.

5. [Impact of corticotropin-releasing hormone on gastrointestinal motility and adrenocorticotropic hormone in normal controls and patients with irritable bowel syndrome](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9691924/) - Fukudo S, Nomura T, Hongo M. *Gut*, 1998 - Small human study (10 people with IBS, 10 controls) showing that CRH increases colon movement, more so in IBS.

6. [Psychological stress and corticotropin-releasing hormone increase intestinal permeability in humans by a mast cell-dependent mechanism](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24153250/) - Vanuytsel T, et al. *Gut*, 2014 - Two small experiments in healthy volunteers showing that public-speaking stress and CRH raise small-intestine permeability, blocked by a mast cell stabilizer. The data in Your Gut Check come from its abstract.

7. [Activated mast cells in proximity to colonic nerves correlate with abdominal pain in irritable bowel syndrome](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14988823/) - Barbara G, et al. *Gastroenterology*, 2004 - Tissue study of 44 people with IBS linking mast cells close to gut nerves with pain severity. A preview of Chapter 8.

8. [A prospective population-based study of triggers of symptomatic flares in IBD](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20372115/) - Bernstein CN, et al. *American Journal of Gastroenterology*, 2010 - Year-long study of 704 people with IBD in Manitoba finding that high perceived stress, but not NSAIDs, antibiotics, or infections, was linked to flares.

9. [Crohn's Disease: Symptoms and Causes](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/crohns-disease/symptoms-causes) - National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK) - The US government's plain-language page on Crohn's disease symptoms and the factors doctors think contribute to it.

10. [The brain-gut pathway in functional gastrointestinal disorders is bidirectional: a 12-year prospective population-based study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22234979/) - Koloski NA, et al. *Gut*, 2012 - Australian study following about 1,000 adults for 12 years, showing that anxiety predicted new gut disorders and gut disorders predicted later anxiety and depression.

11. [Postnatal microbial colonization programs the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal system for stress response in mice](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1664925/) - Sudo N, et al. *Journal of Physiology*, 2004 - The germ-free mouse study analyzed in this chapter's claim box. The free full text includes group sizes and funding.

12. [Effect of Brain-Gut Behavioral Treatments on Abdominal Pain in Irritable Bowel Syndrome: Systematic Review and Network Meta-Analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38777133/) - Goodoory VC, et al. *Gastroenterology*, 2024 - Combines 42 randomized trials (5,220 participants) of CBT, hypnotherapy, and related therapies for IBS pain. The source for "What's new in research" in the At the Bedside case.
