# References: Culture, Food, Family, and Stigma

1. [Understanding the impact of stigma on people with mental illness](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1489832/) - Corrigan PW, Watson AC. *World Psychiatry*, 2002 - A short, free, classic paper that explains public stigma and self-stigma through stereotypes, prejudice, and discrimination. The framework used in Section 19.2.

2. [Impact of perceived stigma on inflammatory bowel disease patient outcomes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2938734/) - Taft TH, Keefer L, et al. *Inflammatory Bowel Diseases*, 2009 - Free full text of the survey of 211 adults with IBD analyzed in this chapter's claim box. Its limitations section is a good model of honest reporting.

3. [A systematic review of disease-related stigmatization in patients living with inflammatory bowel disease](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27022294/) - Taft TH, Keefer L. *Clinical and Experimental Gastroenterology*, 2016 - Reviews research on perceived stigma, self-stigma, and discrimination in IBD, and points out what is still unknown.

4. [What is the impact of mental health-related stigma on help-seeking? A systematic review of quantitative and qualitative studies](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24569086/) - Clement S, et al. *Psychological Medicine*, 2015 - A large review (144 studies, over 90,000 people) showing that stigma discourages seeking mental health care, especially for young people and ethnic minorities.

5. [Unmet Need for Mental Health Services Among High School Students Experiencing Persistent Feelings of Sadness or Hopelessness (2025 Youth Risk Behavior Survey)](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/75/su/su7501a4.htm) - Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, *MMWR Supplements*, October 2026 - The newest national data on teen sadness and hopelessness and on students who needed counseling but didn't get it, broken down by group.

6. [Worldwide incidence and prevalence of inflammatory bowel disease in the 21st century](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29050646/) - Ng SC, et al. *The Lancet*, 2017 - The systematic review of 147 studies showing IBD rising in newly industrialized countries in Asia, Africa, and South America.

7. [Global evolution of inflammatory bowel disease across epidemiologic stages](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12158780/) - Hracs L, et al. *Nature*, 2025 - Free full text of the analysis of 522 studies in 82 regions describing IBD's spread in stages, with maps and figures that are easy to explore.

8. [Epidemiological study of ulcerative proctocolitis in Indian migrants and the indigenous population of Leicestershire](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1379303/) - Probert CS, et al. *Gut*, 1992 - The classic migration study behind the table in Section 19.5. Notice the dates (1972 to 1989) and that it covers one county.

9. [Mental health of South Asian youth in Peel Region, Toronto, Canada: a qualitative study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5695456/) - Islam F, et al. *BMJ Open*, 2017 - Free full text of interviews with 10 South Asian youth about pressures, coping, and barriers to care. A small study that shows how qualitative research describes experiences rather than rates.

10. [School Accommodation Plans and Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)](https://www.crohnscolitisfoundation.org/sites/default/files/2021-06/504plan2021.pdf) - Crohn's & Colitis Foundation, IBD Help Center fact sheet (2021) - A practical guide for families on 504 plans, common accommodations, and how to request them, plus resources for teens.

11. [Talking With Your Doctor](https://medlineplus.gov/talkingwithyourdoctor.html) - MedlinePlus, US National Library of Medicine (updated 2024) - Tips and linked guides for preparing questions, bringing support, using interpreters, and getting the most out of a medical visit.

12. [Health Literacy in Healthy People 2030](https://odphp.health.gov/healthypeople/priority-areas/health-literacy-healthy-people-2030) - Office of Disease Prevention and Health Promotion, US Department of Health and Human Services - Defines personal and organizational health literacy and explains why clear communication is a shared responsibility.
