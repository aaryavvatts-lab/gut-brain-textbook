# For Families

If someone you love lives with Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis, IBS, celiac disease, or another gut condition, you've probably seen how much it affects more than their stomach: their energy, their mood, their plans, and how much they want to talk about it. This page explains how this book can help you understand what they're going through.

## A few things the science says clearly

- **Gut symptoms and mood often travel together, in both directions.** That doesn't mean the symptoms are "all in their head," and it doesn't mean stress caused their disease.
- **Pain can be real even when tests are normal.** In disorders of gut-brain interaction like IBS, the nerves and the brain process gut signals differently.
- **Fatigue during a flare is biological.** Inflammation sends signals to the brain that can cause tiredness and low mood, and anemia, poor sleep, and mood can add to it.
- **Most "gut-healing" products haven't been tested well.** Ask the care team before trying anything.

## How to help

- Ask what kind of support they want before giving advice.
- Keep doctor visits a team effort, and let your teen ask their own questions. The printable [Questions to Ask Your Doctor](appendices/questions-for-your-doctor.md) can help.
- Treat mental health as part of gut care, not separate from it.
- Learn about school accommodations such as a 504 plan (see Chapter 19).

!!! living-with "If you or your teen are struggling right now"
    If someone is thinking about suicide or is in crisis, call or text **988** to reach the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (in the US), or go to the nearest emergency room. Outside the US, you can find a helpline at [findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com/).

This book is for education only and is **not medical advice**. Always follow your care team's guidance.
