# Contributing to Gut Check

Thank you for helping make this free gut-brain neuroscience textbook better. Students, teachers, scientists, clinicians, patients, and families are all welcome.

## Ways to help

- **Report a mistake.** Use the [Report a mistake](https://github.com/aaryavvatts-lab/gut-brain-textbook/issues/new?template=1-error-report.yml) form. Please include a reliable source for the correction if you can.
- **Tell us you used it.** If you used the book in a class, club, or support group, the [I used this book](https://github.com/aaryavvatts-lab/gut-brain-textbook/issues/new?template=2-classroom-use.yml) form helps decide what to improve next.
- **Review a chapter.** If you have expertise in gastroenterology, neuroscience, psychiatry, nutrition, or teaching, or lived experience with a gut condition, autism, or ADHD, the [Volunteer to review a chapter](https://github.com/aaryavvatts-lab/gut-brain-textbook/issues/new?template=4-expert-review.yml) form is the best way to help. Reviewers who agree are credited by name.
- **Suggest an idea**, like new practice questions, simulations, or translations, with the [Suggest an improvement](https://github.com/aaryavvatts-lab/gut-brain-textbook/issues/new?template=3-suggestion.yml) form.

Please don't post your own medical details in a public issue.

## Editing the book yourself

1. Fork the repository and create a branch.
2. Install the tools: `pip install -r requirements.txt`
3. Preview your changes: `mkdocs serve`, then open http://127.0.0.1:8000/gut-brain-textbook/
4. Before you open a pull request, make sure `mkdocs build --strict` finishes with no warnings.
5. Open a pull request that explains what you changed and why, with sources for any factual change.

## Accuracy rules

These rules keep the book trustworthy. Please follow them in every contribution.

- Every statistic, date, drug or device approval, and study claim needs a source you have actually opened. Prefer NIH, CDC, FDA, clinical guidelines, and peer-reviewed research.
- Say whether evidence comes from mice or people, and whether it shows correlation or cause.
- Hype Meter grades must match the evidence. When in doubt, grade lower.
- Never invent a citation, a number, or a quote.
- Approval status must be exact ("approved," "cleared," "authorized," "investigational," and so on).
- No medication doses and no personal medical, diet, or supplement advice.
- Cases are fictional composites.
- Use identity-first language for autism by default, follow individual preferences, and avoid stigmatizing words.
- Don't copy text or figures from copyrighted books.

The full house rules are in [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md) and [EDITOR_DOMAIN.md](EDITOR_DOMAIN.md).

## Writing style

- Write for a high school student with one year of biology. Define every term before you use it.
- Use plain words and short paragraphs. Don't use em dashes.
- Don't change existing headings, because other pages link to them.
